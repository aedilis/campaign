*"... further, the intelligence of our Radiant Path has corroborated this from multiple sources. An armed contingent of your occupiers is advancing on the cache as we speak. They intend to seize it and force you into compliance. I offer our own forces to secure it first—"*

*"Impossible," Governor Sotyria replied curtly. "Rumors of our discussions have made it to the Imperium. They fear our open secession too much to force a confrontation without irrefutable proof, but if they find your forces deployed against them, it will be war."*

*The governor knew the off-world Imperial contingent newly rotated to her planet was a thinly veiled excuse to tamp down any sympathies Kathikos may have for the T'au expansionists. Yet these Imperials were no inquisitors — they had to be practical, first and foremost, and for now that meant leaving her with an exit option. Deny the rumors of her planned sedition, cease contact with the T'au and their talk of strategic alliances and mutual prosperity, and she need not face a traitor's fate. It was an option she would have to preserve as long as she could if she wanted to come out of this with her planet's independence from Imperium and T'au alike, not to mention with with her own life.*

*"We need not indicate any cooperation between us to your oppressors," Ambassador Calmstride offered. If the xenos was bothered by her interruption, he did not show it. He had shown very little to indicate his true feelings ever since she had first allowed him into her tightly controlled court to reopen trade with the defectors of Omadikea. "We have a small team of highly mobile auxiliaries who would be entirely unrecognizable to these interlopers. Even if details of the operation were to become known to their wider bureaucracy, our agents would be identified as independent actors, or even undocumented native fauna. This is a fortuitous opportunity with no true risk to you—"*

*"And a 'fortuitious opportunity' for you to demonstrate just how much we need your alliance, I'm sure," Sotyria cut in with calibrated dismissiveness.*

*The ambassador inclined his head with an easy, natural smile. Only it wasn't natural at all, the governor reminded herself. The xenos expressed themselves through their fingers, not their faces. The expression looked instinctual, but it was a performance, solely intended to make her feel kinship with him. The T'au's overtures were sweeter than the Imperium's threats, but all the same, one didn't maintain her position amidst the viper's nest of Kathikos nobility without recognizing the risk of poison.*

*Sotyria scanned the faces of her court. Any noble who had displayed an unyielding partisanship early on was safely locked in the palace jails — "for security in a moment of acute planetary instability," as she had decreed at the time — so any left free could see the benefits of each side in this unspoken standoff. Or, at least, saw the benefits of pretending they did so before the governor. Each had their bias. Colonel Aspidas of the PDF military command could claim she cared about the security of Kathikos and her forces above all else, but she was a loyalist through and through, barely able to disguise her contempt for the xenos ambassador. Baron Pagonis, wealthy leader of the merchant houses, professed the divinity of the Emperor sincerely, but could hardly conceal his desire to avert Imperial tithes even before he found a generous match in his new T'au trading partners. Every noble in this room had their own private agenda, and it was Sotyria's job to use that tension to keep them all in check and herself balanced on top. She had to act, but could not tip the scales too heavily.*

*Not yet.*

*"Send your dogs," she told the ambassador imperiously. "But when you recover the target, deliver it to this court, not your operating base. Call it an investment in your continued audience here."*

*The T'au envoy swept his hands wide and bowed his head, civility and cooperation emanating from his expression in a way Sotyria strained to remind herself was artificial. "An opportunity we treasure, valued Governor. We would be honored to make you such a gift."*
