*Razvul Upyor of Dire Claw, 47th Assault Company, 17th Great Gun of the Tyrant's Fate*

A concussion, felt more than heard, reverberated across the sub-decks of the macro-cannon. Razvul had put the final hideous touches on trapping the limp figure of the cogitator operator just in time for the second detonation to really capture his attention. “Hrmm, I know we did not bother to bring krak grenades to this fight…” a few backhanded wipes of his blade on the paralysed victim before him spared Razvul’s scabbard the gore left by his unseemly deed. Flashing reticles in Razvul’s lenses confirmed his suspicions, as blinking deathmarks replaced the digital readouts where his squadmates’ heartbeats should have been. “Wasteful. Foolhardy,” Razvul could not be bothered to pick up the slack of his wayward squad, especially for a hunt on their own crew that he advocated against in the first place. Instead, he continued redecorating the station with the remains of the servitors and crew that once populated this room. Consequently, he was not a part of the assault on the adjacent room that his claw leader Iorghu and icon bearer led a moment later. The rest of Dire Claw may throw their lives away on mortals, and Razvul would be pleased to have nothing to do with frontline action.

*A hunt always cleanses weakness from our ranks, bladesman*. Iorghu’s words before the mission had a laughable confidence to them in the face of the unfolding situation. *For one who alleges mastery of the darkness, Razvul, you devote yourself too much to illumination*. Icon Bearer Grunnag’s readout flashed red following the sound of heavy flares through the bulkhead door, and the sonorous concert of violence that proceeded stopped at least one of Iorghu’s hearts a minute later. “Should have listened to Nadru and I, ‘visionary’,” Razvul’s venom was more for the loss of resources than loss of his brothers’ lives.

Ceramite plates and repair cement were not limitless like in the days of the united legion, and reckless hunts into the deck crew only exacerbated the problem. “Some of us have simply gotten too depraved, too indulgent, for their own good,” Razvul paused to consider the iron pitons he used to drive the severed limbs into the wall. Upon second consideration, a barbed wire garland would likely support the rotting flesh longer, “too many forgetting our roots.”

The tapping of mortal boots on decking behind him snapped Razvul out of his task. A quick blink on a targeting rune in his lenses revealed a heat blob behind a far bulkhead. Huh, only one sent to flank? A single mass-reactive round and a viscera strewn hatchway later, and he was back to his grim work. 

“Razvul, you serpent, do you yet draw breath?” Nadru’s voice raked across the vox like a sack of gravel and glass. 

“Against Iorghu’s best intentions, aye.” His masterpiece completed, Razvul stood back to admire the beauty and perfection of the human form. He delighted in how unrecognizable he had made this cogitation station from the machinery it was and the figures now ornamenting its every surface. “Have the rest of my miserable companions completed their flagellant cause?”

“Regretfully, no,” Nadru voxed back. “Iorghu was about half an axe-head away from letting a mortal kill him.” Regretfully indeed, thought Razvul as he skulked into the adjoining room. Plasma scars, fragmentation debris, blood, many crew, and three sets of midnight blue power armor were piled up by the far hatchway. Each dead astartes bore the rough hackmarks of a breacher’s axe, and a shocked visage that death made permanent upon their faces. Nadru held the last living crew three feet off the ground by their neck, progressive kicks each weaker than the ones before.

Taking his time to savor the mortal rictus on their faces, Razvul passed over Grunnag and the other two assault marines pinned against the walls. A quick separation of flesh with his knife revealed that his brothers’ gene-seed were corrupted beyond retrieval. Unworthy…but oh, Iorghu stirred. Razvul’s eyes squinted at the wriggling mass in Nadru’s gauntlets flailing above Iorghu. Curious…

Razvul made for the mortal, knocking them out of Nadru’s grasp which earned a grimace of discontent from his remaining squad member. The look turned to awe as Nadru revealed the assailant’s face beneath the cast away helm. “He’s…a child? HA! Iorghu, do you hear that? You lost us three brothers AND almost died to a gods forsaken child!” Razvul shared in Nadru’s revelries for he appreciated hubris as much as any other jealous underling, however, fate whispered to him most intimately in this moment. A hunt always cleanses weakness from our ranks, bladesman. A foolish mission… a wasteful risk… a leader humbled… and an inhuman amount of fortitude shown by one so young… Yesssss, I was no more than a year older than this one when I was torn open and gifted immortality. “What should we do with the infant?” Nadru asked, his crushing fingers giving away his sadistic hopes.

The sound of Iorghu’s remaining heart was screaming in the mind of Razvul. He could hear the labored effort of blood pushing through the many wounds that likely would seal and begin to heal in the next few moments. With a slow shake of his head and a reverent presentation of his wicked knife, Razvul knelt down next to the wide eyes of Iorghu.

“My dear, dear Nadru,” Razvul tapped his blade’s tip on Iorghu’s neck just above where the progenoid glands. Iorghu’s skin did not bear the same corrupted marks of the others, so his gene-seed must still be viable. Razvul’s skull-faced helm never tore away from his leader’s hateful glare, "we cleanse weakness from our ranks.” He gave a cursory glance to the horrified boy in Nadru’s grasp and to the three fully grown marines who had fatefully underestimated the child. “Do you have any idea of the blessing you are set upon this day,” Razvul checked the boy’s ident badge, “Garid?” Receiving no coherent response, the cruel-smith set his knife to work.
